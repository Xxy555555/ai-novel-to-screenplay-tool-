Chapter 1
Daniel had not seen the old harbor town in seven years. The salt wind still smelled the same as he stepped off the bus, suitcase in hand, the streetlamps flickering against the early dusk. Nothing had changed, and somehow that frightened him more than change would have.
At the corner shop he found Mr. Hale behind the counter, grayer now, his glasses sliding down his nose.
"Daniel," Mr. Hale said slowly, setting down his ledger. "I never thought you'd come back."
"I almost didn't," Daniel admitted. He looked at the dusty shelves, unchanged since his childhood. "How is she?"
Mr. Hale was quiet for a long moment. "She waited," he said at last. "Longer than you deserve."
Daniel lowered his eyes. "I know."
"Then go to her," Mr. Hale said, "before the rain washes the road out."
Daniel paid for a single white candle and stepped back out into the storm, the small flame of an idea already forming in his chest.

Chapter 2
Clara opened the door before he could knock, as if she had been standing behind it for years. The lamplight framed her face, older now but unmistakable.
"You came back," Clara said softly.
"I brought you something," Daniel said. He held out a small wooden box, worn smooth at the corners. Inside lay the silver locket he had taken with him the night he left.
Clara did not reach for it. "You think a gift undoes seven years?"
"No," he said. "I think it begins to."
She studied him in the doorway, the rain dripping from his coat onto the threshold. Somewhere in the house a clock ticked, patient and unhurried.
"Come in," she said at last. "You're letting the cold in."
Daniel stepped inside, and for the first time in seven years the weight in his chest began to lift.

Chapter 3
By morning the storm had passed. Clara made coffee while Daniel sat at the kitchen table, the open locket lying between them.
"Mr. Hale told me you almost left again," Clara said.
"I thought about it," Daniel admitted. "Old habits die hard."
She set a steaming cup before him. "And now?"
"Now I'd like to stay," he said. "If you'll let me earn it."
Later, the three of them walked down to the harbor. Mr. Hale leaned on his cane, watching the fishing boats return with the tide.
"A town remembers everything," the old man said. "The leaving and the coming back. Mostly, it forgives the second."
Clara slipped her hand into Daniel's. "Then we'll give it something worth remembering," she said.
Daniel looked out at the gray water and, for once, did not think of leaving. The gift, he understood now, had never been the locket at all.
